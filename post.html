How I Plan Side Projects Without Burning Out

By Sushanth | April 4, 2026

I used to start side projects with a burst of motivation and then abandon them when the scope expanded. The biggest fix was not better tools, it was better planning. I now write a one-line goal, one success metric, and one clear deadline before writing any code.

From there, I split the work into weekly milestones. Week one is always a minimal version I can publish, even if it feels incomplete. This keeps momentum high and makes progress visible. Once something is live, improving it is much easier than building in private for too long.

I also keep a strict rule for complexity: if a feature needs more than two days, it goes to a later phase. That prevents overengineering and helps me protect energy for consistency. Small finished releases beat perfect unfinished builds every time.

The system is simple, but it changed everything: define a tiny goal, ship quickly, gather feedback, and iterate. If you are stuck with unfinished side projects, try reducing scope by half and publish the first version within seven days.
